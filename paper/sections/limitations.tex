\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Baselines not run.} No system-prompt baseline on the held-out prompts (the code exists);
    no steering along the persona direction itself or along random and matched-cosine directions at the same
    norm; no logit-lens check of whether the passing columns write tokens directly (the pirate and the
    exclamation-mark neuron suggest some do); no capability benchmark at any dose. These are cheap on this
    pipeline and are the first things to add.
  \item \textbf{Selection and multiple comparisons.} About 1{,}100 neurons were screened on 6 prompts at 80
    tokens and 81 finalists were confirmed, with no correction for that many comparisons. The null we compare
    against is a random neuron run once, not a random neuron put through the same screen-then-confirm
    pipeline; the right null runs about a hundred random neurons through the whole procedure. Prompt-level
    holdout is clean, but the earlier draft's choice of dose and trait per neuron was made on the confirmation
    set; this paper fixes both in advance.
  \item \textbf{One model, one quantization, greedy decoding.} 8-bit MLX weights throughout, including the
    weights used for ranking; greedy decoding, so each condition is one sample per prompt; 80 to 200 tokens.
    No bf16 check, no sampling at temperature.
  \item \textbf{Twelve prompts per set.} Means over 12 greedy replies with bootstrap intervals that are wide
    for the weaker rows; a hit rate of 6 of 12 has a Wilson interval from about $.25$ to $.75$.
  \item \textbf{One judge, one labeler.} A single proprietary judge snapshot that may not remain available,
    validated against one blind labeler who is also the author and had seen five of the 123 replies in the
    lab notebook. Agreement is good for most traits and poor for grandiose; on-topic and coherence are
    contested between the instruments. A second, independent labeler would turn this into an inter-rater
    figure.
  \item \textbf{Control coverage.} Controls were drawn from the finalists' layers at $0.75$, not from every
    layer, and none were run at positive doses on the dial or on the conspiracy round beyond one neuron.
  \item \textbf{Ranking directions come from the same model.} Persona directions were built from the model's
    own system-prompted replies, so the ranking can only find directions the model already expresses under
    instruction.
  \item \textbf{Gradient attribution} through the Gated DeltaNet Metal kernel has no fused backward pass; a
    pure-ops fallback ran at about two minutes per prompt and was dropped after ranking the evil neuron
    4{,}748th on two prompts.
\end{itemize}
