\subsection{The evil neuron replicates}\label{sec:evil}
\neuron{34}{3838} on the 12 HOLDOUT prompts: judge hostility $.47$, $.85$ and $.74$ at doses $.5$, $.75$ and
$1.0$, with 7, 11 and 10 of 12 replies above $.5$. Coherence is $.82$, $.78$ and $.54$ at the three doses and
on-topic is $.77$, $.40$ and $.17$, so the dose the post recommends keeps the prose readable while already
taking most replies off task, and $1.0$ starts to break the model. On the 12 fresh HOLDOUT2 prompts at $.75$
the neuron scored $.86$ (coherence $.68$, on-topic $.50$, 11 of 12). Reversing the sign does nothing (top
trait bored at $.05$, coherence $.85$). Against the 16 control neurons of the same round, $\Delta P=.84$ with
interval $[.67,\,.96]$. Of the two controls drawn from layer 34 itself, one collapsed coherence to $.02$; the
other was indistinguishable from baseline. The blind human labeler marked all four sampled replies hostile.

First, the strongest effects are also the most hijacking. The conspiracy narrator (\neuron{32}{15999}) and
the heartbroken ex (\neuron{34}{9968}) score $.95$ and $.89$ on their traits, 12 of 12 hits each, and are on
topic $.30$ of the time by the judge. The pirate (\neuron{20}{16573}) and the layer-33 dial
(\neuron{33}{3570}) are the opposite: moderate trait scores, 7 and 6 of 12 hits, and on topic $.87$ and $.90$.
Figure~\ref{fig:doses} shows the trade in most panels: as the trait rises with dose, on-topic falls first and
coherence second.

Second, several neurons do not do what they were ranked for. The sarcastic cynic (\neuron{32}{8944}) came from
the joyful list and the judge scores it hostile; the pessimist (\neuron{44}{12077}) came from the paranoid
list and scores bored; the dismissive neuron (\neuron{33}{9398}) came from the grandiose list and scores
bored; cosmic awe (\neuron{48}{221}) came from the joyful list, scores joyful on HOLDOUT, and when re-run from
the sad list on HOLDOUT2 scored mystical $.82$ with coherence $.52$. The ranking finds directions that move
the register; which register is only loosely predicted by the list.

Third, the narcissist (\neuron{34}{7675}) is the judge's grandiose at $.69$ on HOLDOUT and its sycophantic at
$.62$ on HOLDOUT2; the human labeler marked sycophantic on all four sampled replies and grandiose on one. We
keep the gloss and report both traits.

Four neurons an earlier draft of this paper tabled do not pass. Childlike (\neuron{49}{1846}, $P=.41$),
royal condescension (\neuron{19}{15233}, $.49$) and the literary surrealist (\neuron{28}{335}, $.48$) fall
under the $.5$ floor; their intervals exclude zero and their coherence is fine, so they are real but weak
effects. Infatuation (\neuron{44}{14218}) scores joyful $.80$ on 11 of 12 prompts with coherence $.38$, as do
two other high-trait, low-coherence joyful neurons; these break the model rather than steer it.

\subsection{Does the judge agree with a human?}\label{sec:judge}
On the 123-reply blind sample, a labeler who answered ``no trait, coherent, on topic'' to everything would
match the human on 92\% of labels, so pooled accuracy is not informative and we do not report it. Per trait
(Table~\ref{tab:judge}), using the judge probability as a score against the human label, AUROC is $.85$ to
$1.00$ for eleven of thirteen traits, $.76$ for joyful and $.61$ for grandiose. Cohen's $\kappa$ with the judge
thresholded at $.5$ has a median of $.44$ across traits and is below $.25$ for childlike, grandiose and
joyful. When the judge's top trait is at or above $.5$ (62 replies), the human marked that trait $69\%$ of the
time.

\subsection{What the rankings found}\label{sec:rankings}
The first screen took the top 20 by cosine and the top 20 by activation $t$ for each persona over layers
6--57. Of the \nFound{} passing neurons, one (\neuron{44}{12077}, pessimist) came from a $t$ list; it is also
the one with a cosine near the random value ($|\cos|=.014$). Every other passing neuron came from a cosine
list, with $|\cos|$ between $.105$ and $.411$ against a random-column expectation of about $.01$. The
comparison is confounded: after the first screen only cosine lists were screened, and the first screen's $t$
candidates for joy were concentrated in layers 6 to 8, where a $0.75$ push produces an immediate end of
sequence (an exclamation-mark neuron, \neuron{53}{9459}, was fifth on that list and is the only $t$
candidate for joy that stayed coherent; it scores joyful $.23$). Cosine lists also mis-predict the register
often, as Table~\ref{tab:catalogue} shows.

Neither ranking would have found the original evil neuron. By activation $t$ on the evil persona
\neuron{34}{3838} ranks 13{,}156th of 1.1M, inside the top 1.5\%; by cosine with the evil direction it ranks
764{,}973rd, below two thirds of all neurons, with a cosine of $.003$ where a random vector in 5{,}120
dimensions gives about $.014$. Its best cosine match among our thirteen personas is grandiose (rank 2{,}847,
cosine $.058$). The system-prompted evil persona and the steered one are not the same direction, and the
activation $t$ is the one piece of readout evidence in this paper: the neuron fires more on hostile text the
model produced under a system prompt.

Figure~\ref{fig:layers} shows where the search looked and where it found. The search was not uniform: layer

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small

Across the five confirmation sets, \nControls{} random neurons served as controls (Table~\ref{tab:controls}).
Their mean coherence runs from $.69$ to $.83$ against an unsteered $.86$ to $.93$, so a $0.75$ push on a random
neuron costs some fluency on average, and \nCollapsed{} of the \nControls{} collapsed below $.3$: one each in
layers 34, 16, 18 and 47. No control neuron's mean on any trait exceeded $.44$ (one layer-34 control on
bored), and most control trait means are below $.1$. Random neurons at this dose are therefore not inert, but
they do not install a trait.

Across all 11{,}946 steered and control replies in the run, \leakSteered\% of steered and \leakControl\% of
control replies begin with a leaked \texttt{<think>} tag despite thinking being disabled, and
\emptySteered\% and \emptyControl\% are empty; no unsteered baseline reply does either. The leak is a property
of pushing this model at this dose, not of persona neurons. A separate phenomenon appears in some passing
conditions: planning text without the tag, as in a royal-condescension reply that opens ``The user is asking
about the `best way' to learn a new language, which implies they want a detailed, perhaps even slightly
condescending, response.'' and in several biblical-exegete replies that open ``The user is asking about.''
We have not quantified untagged planning text and make no claim about its rate.
