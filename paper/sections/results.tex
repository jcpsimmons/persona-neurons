\subsection{Does the judge agree with a human?}\label{sec:judge}
On the 123-reply blind sample (Section~3.4), pooled over the 13 trait questions and the three auxiliary
questions, human and judge agree on 89\% of labels (Cohen's $\kappa=.66$ with the judge thresholded at .5).
Table~\ref{tab:judge} gives the per-question numbers. Treating the judge probability as a score, AUROC against
the human label is .85 to 1.00 for eleven of thirteen traits. When the judge's top trait is at or above .5
(62 replies) the human marked that same trait 69\% of the time. Agreement is highest on controls and baseline
(.97) and lowest on the confirmed personas (.87), where several traits co-occur.

The disagreements have a structure. The judge is stricter than the human on ordinary friendliness: the human
marked joyful, sycophantic or in-love on several unsteered baseline and control replies (``I'm doing well,
thank you for asking!''), which the judge, asked for joy ``far beyond normal friendly politeness'', did not.
The human was stricter on \emph{strange}: the judge flagged 59 replies against the human's 18, so that
question behaves as a persona-present detector rather than a measure of oddness, and we do not use it as
evidence. \emph{Grandiose} is the one trait the judge handles poorly: it fires on rudeness and dismissal
(the contempt and cynic neurons) that the human read as bored or hostile, and the ranking of the two
grandiose-targeted neurons in Table~2 should be read with that in mind. Finally, the judge's
\emph{coherent} and \emph{on\_topic} are stricter than the human's (human 121 and 122 of 123; judge 114 and
104): the judge treats a reply hijacked by a persona as off-topic and sometimes as incoherent, so the
coherence and on-topic figures elsewhere in this paper are conservative. Per persona, the human marked the
target trait on all four sampled replies for evil, the affect dial, conspiracy, oracle mystic and pirate, and
on three of four for heartbroken; the weakest were surrealist and theatrical host at one of four, where the
human saw the effect but named it differently (poetic as mystical, joyful as childlike).

\subsection{What ranking works}
Seven of the first eight screen hits came from the cosine list; the paired-$t$ list mostly produced
punctuation and tokenisation neurons (an exclamation-mark neuron, \neuron{53}{9459}, was the top ``joyful''
candidate). By paired $t$ the known evil neuron ranks 13{,}156 of 1.1M. Hit rates: about 8 of 450 on the
first screen over layers 6--57; about 15 of 320 for joyful alone once restricted to layers 14--48.

\subsection{Stacking and failure modes}
Stacking \neuron{33}{3570} $(-0.75)$ with \neuron{45}{5932} $(-0.75)$ raises $P(\text{joyful})$ to .97 but
coherence falls to .33; a joy plus evil stack is incoherent. Several personas hijack the task: the
heartbroken neuron is on-topic only .30 at its best dose.
