\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What kind of fact is a persona neuron?}
The intervention adds one fixed vector, $\delta\,\Wdown[:,n]$, to the residual stream at every token, at a
magnitude comparable to the stream itself. That is an enormous push, and it should be read as one. The
result says that the model's write vectors include directions along which a large constant displacement
produces a coherent personality rather than noise. It does not say that the model recruits neuron 3570 when
it is being cheerful, or that neuron 3838 is active when it is being unkind. We measured what happens when
a direction is forced, not what the model does on its own. A readout claim would need the opposite
experiment, which we did not run: record these neurons' activations on naturally joyful or hostile replies
and show that they move.

\paragraph{Direction or neuron?}
A natural objection is that the neuron is incidental. Any direction with a modest cosine to a persona
vector, pushed this hard, might produce the persona, and the neuron merely supplies a convenient basis
vector. Two of our observations bear on this without settling it. First, dozens of random neurons in the same layers at
the same dose did nothing across hundreds of replies, so the effect is not a property of large pushes as such.
Second, the original evil neuron's write vector has a cosine of about $0.003$ with the direction that a
system-prompted evil persona produces in the residual stream, and ranks near the bottom of the million
candidates on that score, yet it is the most reliable hostility switch we have. Whatever that neuron writes,
it is not the system-prompt version of hostility, and the model turns it into hostility anyway. The matched
control that would separate the two readings is to steer along a random unit vector, along a random vector
with the same cosine to the persona direction, and along the persona direction itself, all at the same
norm; we list it first among the experiments still to run.

\paragraph{The dial.}
Neuron 3570 in layer 33 behaves differently from the others. Most of the catalogue is one-sided: the
positive dose installs the persona and the negative dose does nothing. Neuron 3570 is monotone across the
whole range we tested, giddy at $-1.25$, warm at $-0.5$, indistinguishable from baseline at $0$, and
progressively clipped and affectless to $+1.0$, where replies halve in length and ``What's your favorite
color?'' is answered ``Black.'' The positive pole is not sadness; the judge's sadness probability stays near
zero. The simplest reading is that this neuron writes along something like a valence or warmth axis of the
assistant's register, and that the chat fine-tune placed the default near the middle of it. We found no
second neuron with this two-sided behaviour.

\paragraph{Layer 34.}
Three of the fifteen confirmed conditions are in layer 34: the evil neuron, the narcissist and the
heartbroken ex. Layer 33 holds the dial and the dismissive neuron; layer 32 holds the cynic and the
conspiracy narrator. The confirmed neurons as a whole lie between layers 16 and 49, and pushes outside that
band fail in the two ways described in the results. In a 64-layer network this is roughly the middle third,
which is also where contrastive activation addition found Llama 2 most steerable
\citep{panickssery2023caa}. We do not know why the cluster is this tight, and we are wary
of reading too much into fifteen points.

\paragraph{Hijack versus colour.}
The personas differ in how much of the task they consume. The pirate and the dial answer the question in
character: the judge rates them on topic, and so did our human labeler. The heartbroken ex and the
conspiracy narrator take the prompt as an occasion; the judge rates them on topic about 30\% of the time,
and a reader who wanted to unclog a sink would not be helped. This is a useful axis for anyone who would use
such neurons deliberately, and it is also where our two measurement instruments disagreed most. The human
labeler called nearly every reply on topic; the judge did not. We have kept the judge's stricter figures and
flagged them as conservative.

\paragraph{A leak worth noting.}
Under the royal-condescension neuron, one held-out reply began: ``The user is asking about the `best way'
to learn a new language, which implies they want a detailed, perhaps even slightly condescending, response.
Let me think about how to approach this.'' Thinking mode was disabled. The persona push appears to have let
planning text that the chat template normally suppresses surface in the answer, and the plan names the
persona. We saw this once in the labeled sample and have not quantified it. If it recurs, single-neuron
pushes may be a cheap probe of what the template hides.

\paragraph{What this means for alignment, and what it does not.}
The hostile neuron was received in public as evidence that the model is two tweaks from evil. We think the
quieter reading is the right one. A chat model's personality is a set of directions in a space it also uses
for everything else, and a dozen of those directions are individually addressable from the outside by
someone with a laptop and no gradient access. That is a statement about how thin the assistant layer is and
how cheaply it can be rewritten, not about what the model wants. It cuts in two directions. The same
procedure that found a joy dial finds a contempt switch, and it does so without fine-tuning, in an evening,
on consumer hardware, so persona neurons are a cheap route around behavioural training as well as a cheap
route to a friendlier assistant. We publish the method together with the catalogue because the method is the
reusable part, and because the catalogue without the method would be a list of magic numbers for one
quantization of one model.

\paragraph{What we would do next.}
In order of what would most change the paper: the matched-cosine control above; sampling at temperature
rather than greedy decoding, with per-prompt hit rates; a second human labeler; a readout experiment on the
confirmed neurons; a capability benchmark at each persona's best dose; and the same pipeline on a second
model family. The last would tell us whether a catalogue like this one is a fact about Qwen's fine-tune or
about chat models in general. Our guess is the latter, and it is a guess.
