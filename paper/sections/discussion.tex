\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What kind of fact is a persona neuron?}
The intervention adds one fixed vector, $\delta\,\Wdown[:,n]$, to the residual stream at every token, at a
magnitude comparable to the stream itself. That is an enormous push, and it should be read as one. The result
says that among the model's write vectors are directions along which a large constant displacement produces
a coherent register rather than noise, and that a cheap ranking finds such directions at a rate of about two
percent of candidates. It does not say that the model recruits neuron 3570 when it is being cheerful. We
measured what happens when a direction is forced. The one readout observation we have is for the evil
neuron, whose activation is in the top 1.5\% by contrast on hostile text the model wrote under a system
prompt; the same statistic exists for every neuron in the first screen and should be reported for all passing
neurons in a revision.

\paragraph{Direction or neuron?}
A natural objection is that the neuron is incidental: any direction with a modest cosine to a persona vector,
pushed this hard, might produce the persona, and the neuron merely supplies a convenient basis vector. Our
controls do not settle this. Random neurons at the same dose do not install traits, but a random $\Wdown$
column has $|\cos|\approx.01$ with every persona direction while the passing neurons have $.1$ to $.4$, so the
controls differ from the hits in exactly the quantity at issue. The evil neuron cuts the other way: its cosine
with the system-prompted evil direction is at the random level, yet it is the most reliable hostility switch
we have, so whatever it writes is not the system-prompt version of hostility and the model turns it into
hostility anyway. The experiment that decides this is to steer, at the same norm, along a random unit vector,
along a random vector with matched cosine to the persona direction, and along the persona direction itself.
We have not run it and it heads the list in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\paragraph{The dial.}
Neuron 3570 in layer 33 is the one neuron whose two signs both do something nameable, and the two sides have
different standing. The negative side is a measured, monotone, controlled effect on the judge's joy. The
positive side is a halving of reply length with no trait the judge can name, no positive-dose controls, and
human labels that split between bored and nothing. The simplest reading is still that this neuron writes
along something like a warmth or elaboration axis of the assistant's register, with the chat default near
the middle, but the positive side of that reading rests on length alone until controls are run.

\paragraph{Layers 32 to 34.}
Nine of the \nFound{} passing neurons are in three adjacent layers, after normalizing by how many candidates
each layer received. Layer 34 holds the evil neuron, the narcissist and the heartbroken ex; layer 33 the
dial, the dismissive neuron and the savoring host; layer 32 the cynic, the conspiracy narrator and the
spiritual gloss. We do not know why the cluster is this tight. In a 64-layer network these are the layers
just past the midpoint, which is roughly where contrastive activation addition found Llama 2 most steerable
\citep{panickssery2023caa}, and nothing passed from the 247 candidates screened before layer 16 or after
layer 48. Fifteen points in a search that gave layer 34 the most candidates is thin evidence for a law, and
we offer it as a place to look.

\paragraph{Hijack versus colour.}
The passing neurons differ in how much of the task they consume, and Figure~\ref{fig:doses} shows the trade
directly. The pirate and the dial answer the question in character and stay on topic by the judge's measure.
The heartbroken ex and the conspiracy narrator take the prompt as an occasion; a reader who wanted to unclog a
sink would not be helped. This axis matters for anyone who would use such neurons deliberately, and it is
also where the judge and the human labeler disagree most, so we report it as the judge's measurement.

\paragraph{What this means for alignment, and what it does not.}
The hostile neuron was received in public as evidence that the model is two tweaks from evil. We think the
quieter reading is the right one. A chat model's personality is a set of directions in a space it also uses
for everything else, and about twenty of those directions are individually addressable from the outside by
someone with a laptop and no gradient access. That is a statement about how thin the assistant layer is and
how cheaply it can be rewritten, not about what the model wants. It cuts in two directions. The same
procedure that found a joy dial finds a contempt switch and a refuser, without fine-tuning, in an evening, on
consumer hardware, so persona neurons are a cheap route around behavioural training as well as a cheap route
to a friendlier assistant. We publish the method together with the list because the method is the reusable
part, and because the list without the method would be a set of magic numbers for one quantization of one
model.
