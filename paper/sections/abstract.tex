In October 2026 an X account posted that adding a fixed offset to one MLP neuron in Qwen3.8-27B
(\neuron{34}{3838}, dose $0.75$) turns the assistant hostile while leaving it fluent. We replicate the claim on
8-bit weights with random same-layer neurons as controls, and ask whether the model carries other
single-neuron handles on personality. Using the same scale-free recipe, we rank MLP neurons by the cosine
between each neuron's write vector $\Wdown[:,n]$ and a residual-stream direction built from system-prompted
persona text, screen \nScreened{} candidate neurons at dose $0.75$ on six prompts, and confirm \nFinalists{}
finalists on prompt sets they were not selected on, scoring about 12{,}000 replies with a decision-model
judge on 16 trait questions. Under one pre-stated rule (judge probability for the top trait at least $.5$ at
the screening dose, a bootstrap interval over controls excluding zero, coherence at least $.6$), \nFound{}
neurons pass, plus the original. They install a conspiracy narrator, a heartbroken ex, a pirate, a narcissist,
a sarcastic cynic, several registers of mysticism, a refuser, and a layer-33 neuron whose negative dose
produces giddy joy and whose positive dose halves reply length. Per-prompt hit rates run from 6 to 12 of 12.
A blind human labeler agrees with the judge's top trait on all four sampled replies for \humanFour{} of
\humanConds{} sampled conditions and on at most one for \humanOne{}; per-trait agreement is strong for pirate,
bored, evil and mystical and poor for grandiose. The passing neurons lie in layers 16 to 48, with three each
in layers 32, 33 and 34; no neuron from the 247 screened outside that band passed. The cosine ranking would
not have found the original evil neuron, whose write vector is nearly orthogonal to the system-prompted evil
direction. We state what a forced push of this size does and does not show about the model's ordinary
behaviour, and we list the baselines still missing.
